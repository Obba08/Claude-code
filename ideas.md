# Fingertip Facts: video ideas

Every video uses the same shape: start on something everyone recognises (ideally a fingertip touching it),
zoom into the detail that explains it, pull back to the start. Same style, voice, music and template.

Marked ♻️ = reuses pictures we already have (fewer new pictures, lower cost).

## Series 1: Your body, explained

| # | Title | Starts on | Zooms into |
|---|-------|-----------|------------|
| 1 | Why a paper cut hurts so much ♻️ | A paper edge on a fingertip | Fingertips are packed with nerve endings → signal travels up a nerve to the brain |
| 2 | How a scab forms ♻️ (sequel to the splinter) | A small cut on a finger | Platelets plug the gap → a protein mesh (fibrin) traps blood cells → new skin grows underneath |
| 3 | How fingerprints form | A fingerprint on glass | Ridges form in the womb as skin layers grow; no two are the same, even in identical twins |
| 4 | Why you get goosebumps ♻️ | Arm hair standing up | A tiny muscle at the base of each hair pulls it upright |
| 5 | How a vaccine trains your body ♻️ (sequel) | A plaster on an arm | A harmless piece of a germ → B cells learn it → memory cells stay ready |
| 6 | What a fever is for | A thermometer | The brain raises the body's set temperature to help fight infection |
| 7 | How touch reaches your brain | A finger touching a surface | Touch sensors in skin → nerve → spinal cord → brain |

## Topics to handle carefully

Some popular "facts" are still debated, so they'd break the "only well-known facts" rule. For example:
why fingers wrinkle in water (the mechanism is known, the reason is debated), why we yawn, and how plane
wings make lift (often explained wrongly). Skip them, or only cover the parts that are settled.

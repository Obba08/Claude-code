# Food vs Water: How Long Can You Last? — Script

**Format:** Vertical 9:16 · about 1:20 · narrator voiceover · ~150 words
**Journey:** Freezer and a glass of water → into the body (liver, fat, blood, kidney) → pull back out to the freezer and glass, ready to loop.

---

## 1. Narration (word for word)

A freezer full of food feels like safety. But without water, it won't keep you alive for long.

Here's the rough rule. Without food, most people can last weeks. Without water? Usually just a few days.

So why the huge gap?

Your body packs its own food. First, it burns sugar stored in your liver. When that runs out, it switches to fat. And most of us carry plenty. Fat is basically backup fuel, built for exactly this.

But water? Your body has no backup tank. You lose it all day, just by breathing, sweating and peeing. Without a refill, your blood gets thicker and harder to pump. Your kidneys can't flush out waste properly. And within days, your body starts shutting down.

That's the incredible part. You can go weeks without a meal, but only days without a drink.

So go ahead, stock the freezer. Just don't forget the water.

*(151 words)*

## Notes before the next step

- **Facts.** "Weeks without food, days without water" is the widely taught rule of thumb (often said as "3 weeks / 3 days"). Real times vary a lot with heat, activity, age and health, so the script keeps it loose and uses no exact numbers. The liver-sugar → fat order and the effects of dehydration (thicker blood, kidneys struggling) are standard physiology.
- **The trend.** I couldn't find a clear "freezer hack" story that matches the hook. What does show up is Europe's frozen-food boom and people running out of freezer space. The opening line works for either, so it doesn't claim anything specific about the trend.
- **Loop.** The last shot lands on the same freezer frame as the opening, so it can loop cleanly.

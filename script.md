# How the Immune System Works — Script

Format: vertical 9:16 · about 1:30 · one calm narrator · about 215 words

---

## 1. Narration (word for word)

1. A tiny splinter in your finger sets off your immune system.
2. Your skin is a wall that keeps germs out.
3. The splinter just punched a hole in it, and bacteria ride in on the wood.
4. Damaged cells release chemical signals.
5. Nearby blood vessels widen, so more blood flows in.
6. That's why the spot turns red, warm and swollen.
7. White blood cells squeeze out of the vessels and follow the signals.
8. Neutrophils usually arrive first and swallow bacteria whole.
9. Bigger cells called macrophages eat germs and clean up the mess.
10. Often, that's enough.
11. But a dendritic cell also grabs pieces of the germ and carries them to a lymph node, a small bean-shaped station.
12. There, the pieces are checked against a huge crowd of immune cells.
13. Only a few B cells fit this exact germ.
14. When one fits, it copies itself again and again.
15. The copies make antibodies, Y-shaped proteins that stick to this one germ.
16. Antibodies travel through the blood and coat the bacteria.
17. A coated germ is easier for the eating cells to find.
18. The first time, this takes days.
19. But some B cells stay behind as memory cells.
20. If the same germ comes back, the response is faster and stronger.
21. That's the idea behind vaccines.
22. Back at your finger, the fight is over, and the wall is whole again.

## 4. Style line

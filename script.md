# Apoptosis — Video Script

Format: vertical 9:16 · about 1:30 · around 250 words of narration

---

## 1. Narration (word for word)

The gaps between your fingers were made by cells that killed themselves.

Before you were born, your hand looked like a paddle.

Your fingers were joined by a thin web of tissue.

Then the cells in that web got a signal.

And they shut themselves down. On purpose.

This is called apoptosis. Programmed cell death.

Here's how a cell does it.

Inside are mitochondria, the parts that make the cell's energy.

When the signal comes, they release a protein called cytochrome c.

That switches on enzymes called caspases.

Caspases work like scissors. They cut the cell apart from the inside.

The cell shrinks. Its DNA gets chopped into pieces.

Then it breaks into small, neat packages, each wrapped in membrane.

The name comes from a Greek word for falling off, like leaves from a tree.

Nothing spills out, so there's no inflammation. No mess.

A cleaner cell called a macrophage comes along and eats the pieces.

And it's not just your fingers.

Your body uses this to remove cells that are damaged, infected, or no longer needed.

Billions of your cells do it every day.

When it fails, damaged cells can survive and keep dividing. That's one of the ways cancer starts.

So every time you spread your fingers, you're looking at cells that knew when to leave.

## 2. Shot list

One continuous camera move: hand → web between fingers → one cell → inside the cell → back out to the hand.

**1. "The gaps between your fingers were made by cells that killed themselves."**
- On screen: `hand.png`, open hand, fingers spread, centered.
- Camera: still, then a slow push toward the gap between the index and middle finger.

**2. "Before you were born, your hand looked like a paddle."**
- On screen: `embryo_hand.png` fades in over the hand, same position and size, so the fingers seem to melt into a paddle.
- Camera: keeps the same slow push.

**3. "Your fingers were joined by a thin web of tissue."**
- On screen: the web between two finger ridges. Label "web of tissue" appears.
- Camera: pushes deeper into the web.

**4. "Then the cells in that web got a signal."**
- On screen: the web's surface turns into `cell.png`, one cell filling the frame. A soft blue pulse travels in from the edge and touches the cell.
- Camera: continues forward with no cut. The cell grows out of the texture.

**5. "And they shut themselves down. On purpose."**
- On screen: the cell's blue rim fades out. Text: "on purpose."
- Camera: brief hold.

**6. "This is called apoptosis. Programmed cell death."**
- On screen: title text "APOPTOSIS", then the subtitle "programmed cell death."
- Camera: slow drift.

**7. "Here's how a cell does it."**
- On screen: the cell, with the nucleus and mitochondria visible.
- Camera: pushes through the cell membrane.

**8. "Inside are mitochondria, the parts that make the cell's energy."**
- On screen: close-up of the mitochondria inside the cell. Label "mitochondria."
- Camera: glides toward one mitochondrion.

**9. "When the signal comes, they release a protein called cytochrome c."**
- On screen: small blue specks drift out of the mitochondria. Label "cytochrome c."
- Camera: follows the specks outward.

**10. "That switches on enzymes called caspases."**
- On screen: the blue specks spread through the cell like a wave. Label "caspases."
- Camera: pulls back slightly to show the whole cell.

**11. "Caspases work like scissors. They cut the cell apart from the inside."**
- On screen: blue lines flicker across the cell's interior like small cuts. A small scissors icon is drawn by the animation.
- Camera: hold on the whole cell.

**12. "The cell shrinks. Its DNA gets chopped into pieces."**
- On screen: the cell scales down. The nucleus darkens and splits into fragments. Label "DNA."
- Camera: pushes in a little to keep the shrinking cell large in frame.

**13. "Then it breaks into small, neat packages, each wrapped in membrane."**
- On screen: the cell crossfades into `cell_fragments.png`, a cluster of small round bubbles in the same spot.
- Camera: slight pull back.

**14. "The name comes from a Greek word for falling off, like leaves from a tree."**
- On screen: the fragments drift slowly downward like falling leaves. Text: "apo = off · ptosis = falling."
- Camera: tilts down gently with them.

**15. "Nothing spills out, so there's no inflammation. No mess."**
- On screen: the fragments settle, each sealed. Text "no inflammation."
- Camera: hold.

**16. "A cleaner cell called a macrophage comes along and eats the pieces."**
- On screen: `macrophage.png` slides in from the side, wraps around the fragments, and they disappear into it. Label "macrophage."
- Camera: tracks the macrophage.

**17. "And it's not just your fingers."**
- On screen: the macrophage moves off. Empty space opens where the web was.
- Camera: the pull back starts.

**18. "Your body uses this to remove cells that are damaged, infected, or no longer needed."**
- On screen: three words appear one by one: "damaged · infected · not needed."
- Camera: keeps pulling back. The cell-level view becomes tissue again.

**19. "Billions of your cells do it every day."**
- On screen: many tiny cell shapes across the frame. A few blink out softly in blue. Text: "billions / day."
- Camera: keeps pulling back.

**20. "When it fails, damaged cells can survive and keep dividing. That's one of the ways cancer starts."**
- On screen: one cell stays when it should vanish, then copies itself, 1 → 2 → 4, using copies of `cell.png`.
- Camera: brief pause in the pull back to focus on the dividing cells.

**21. "So every time you spread your fingers, you're looking at cells that knew when to leave."**
- On screen: the tissue resolves back into the gap between the fingers. `embryo_hand.png` turns back into `hand.png`, and the fingers spread.
- Camera: full pull back to the exact opening frame, so the video loops.

## 3. Picture list

1. **hand.png**: An adult human hand, palm facing the viewer, fingers spread wide, cut off at the wrist. Nothing else in the picture.
2. **embryo_hand.png**: An embryonic hand, a rounded paddle with five faint finger ridges joined by thin webbing, cut off at the wrist. Same angle and size as hand.png so they can crossfade.
3. **cell.png**: One healthy round animal cell, seen in cut-away 3D. It shows the membrane, a clear nucleus, and several bean-shaped mitochondria inside.
4. **cell_fragments.png**: A loose cluster of about eight small, round, membrane-wrapped bubbles, the remains of one cell. Some hold dark bits of nucleus.
5. **macrophage.png**: One large, irregular immune cell with soft, reaching arm-like extensions, seen in 3D.

All pictures: a single object on a plain white background, with no labels, text, numbers or arrows.
